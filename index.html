About

Software that listens first

Masa is a steady, private space for her body's story, whatever season it's in. Some women come mid-IVF. Some come because they've just started paying attention to their own body for the first time. Some aren't trying for anything at all, and may never be. Masa meets her wherever she is, without nudging her toward a goal she didn't choose.

The journey deserves software that never rushes. Masa is built slowly, with care, by people who have lived these seasons themselves — and built to grow alongside her, one season at a time.

She matters. Her journey matters. And it is uniquely hers.

Why I built Masa

A letter from our founder

BS
Bella S.
Founder & CEO, Masa

I built Masa at 4 AM, which is when most of it was needed.

That is the hour I learned my body's language best and trusted it least. The hour I'd reopen the same app I'd checked at midnight to see whether the number had changed, knowing it hadn't. The hour I'd search a symptom I already knew the answer to, because searching felt like doing something.

Two days after an embryo transfer, I woke at 4 AM with the worst cramp. I walked into the living room, one hand on the mantel, then to my knees, the other arm around my stomach. I started praying with tears coming down my cheeks. Then I opened my app, and nothing there said anything about my experience.

The next day, I went in for my scheduled scan to see how things were progressing. I got the news I had been dreading, even if some part of me had already anticipated it. Afterwards, I opened the app again, and still found nothing that would hold me and my experience.

I felt so alone.

My road ran through trying to conceive naturally, then multiple rounds of IVF — retrievals, transfers. It included multiple losses — chemical, blighted ovum, missed miscarriage — with names I learned too well. It included embryos on ice and the strange arithmetic of loving something that is not with you and cannot be hurried. It included days I was fine and days I was not, often the same day.

What I did not have was an app that could hold any of it.

The ones I tried were built for a different woman. They assumed conception was a matter of timing and effort, that a positive test was the end of the story, that I wanted a flame emoji for logging my temperature seven days running. They congratulated me before I was ready to be congratulated. They used language as though what was happening to my body were a matter of sprinkles and luck. It sat in an app I paid for.

They were also confident in a way my doctors never were. My RE would say this is progress, and it's early. The app would say don't worry.

One of those was honest.

I spent my career in health tech, most of it moving clinical data between systems that were never built to speak to each other. I knew what careless software does to a person at the worst moment of her life. I was watching it happen to me.

And eventually I understood that the problem was bigger than fertility, bigger than IVF, and bigger than any one app.

Too much technology starts by deciding what a woman should want, then builds the experience around getting her there. Try to conceive. Get pregnant. Have the baby. Move on. Her body becomes a series of milestones, and the software decides what counts as progress.

I wanted to build something that started somewhere else: with her.

With what she is living through today. With what she wants — or doesn't want. With what she knows, what she doesn't know yet, and what she isn't ready to name.

She matters. Her journey matters. And it is uniquely hers.

Technology should understand her without deciding who she is, what she wants, or where she goes next.

That belief became Masa.

So the rule underneath Masa is simple, and it never bends: acknowledge what she's living through, support her without prescribing, and never dismiss. No streaks. No confetti on a positive. No "just relax." No false certainty.

A positive beta and a bleed get the same dignity, because on the day either one arrives, she deserves the same steadiness from her phone.

Sometimes what she needs isn't another answer. It's a place to be for a moment. That's part of why Masa has Today: a daily message that meets her where she is, space to journal what she's carrying, and a simple way to notice the signs her body is giving her. Not everything needs to become a prediction, a score, or a task. Some things just need somewhere to belong.

The rest follows from that. Masa shows the number — beta, doubling time, follicle count, blast grade — big and steady, because those numbers matter deeply to the woman reading them, and hiding them is condescension.

Her journey in Masa belongs to her. Masa doesn't advance her to a new stage because a calculation decided she must be there. She chooses when her life moves. Nothing about her season is decided for her.

And she doesn't have to be in the hard part to be here.

Some women come to Masa mid-IVF. Some come because they've started paying attention to their own body for the first time and want to understand what it's doing. Some aren't trying for anything at all, and may never be. Masa won't quietly turn into something else — no countdown to conception, no nudge toward a goal she didn't choose.

What she shouldn't have to do is leave.

Every time a woman's life changes, she gets handed to a different product and starts from zero. One app for her cycle, another for trying, another for pregnancy, another for the baby, and nothing at all for the seasons in between.

Her history doesn't restart when her life changes.

Neither does she.

It shouldn't sit split across five companies who each know one chapter of her and none of the rest. Masa is being built differently: to stay with her as her life changes, without assuming what her next season should be.

Not every season is here yet. I'm saying so plainly because I'd rather build what we have promised with care than pretend we're further along than we are — and because a woman handing me this much of herself deserves to know the difference between what exists and what's coming.

And Masa never sells her.

No ads, in any tier, ever. We don't sell her personal data. That is not a feature we might revisit at scale — it is part of the promise. An app that knows this much about a woman's body and then monetizes her attention has already broken it.

I am also a mother of a toddler now. Masa carries all of it — the trying, the waiting, the grief, the joy, and the ordinary days in between — because one body's story can hold many seasons.

Masa began with what I needed at 4 AM.

I'm building it for whatever hour she needs it.

Every season, yours.
